# Baseline audit for 2.0 — 2026-11-10

Status: baseline. Four independent read-only reviews of `main` at `c4b1496` (four commits behind the release at the time; the missing commits added provider sign-out), against the KofTwentyTwo standards at revision `707bbd33f0b9` (2026-11-08). This file is the rubric and the starting point; it is updated as findings close, and the re-audit is appended at the end.

## The release rule

2.0 is done only when fresh reviewers, using the rubric below, score 9 or better on every criterion in the three code areas: Core, Windows, and the companion, command line and service host. Those eighteen criteria are what "code, design, architecture and security" means here; they include test quality and accessibility, because those are part of the code. The six Repository criteria are scored and reported in the same re-audit and are not part of the rule: several depend on CI, which is owned by separate work.

## How to re-run it

- One reviewer per area, read-only, on the head of `release/2.0` at the time; the re-audit records that commit and the standards revision it used.
- A reviewer is given the sections from here down to the end of "Rubric", and nothing after it: not the baseline scores, and not the findings and their statuses, until their own scores and findings are written.
- Each reads the standards first (`coding/README.md`, `coding/csharp-dotnet.md`, `testing.md`; the repository reviewer also reads `ci-cd.md`, `repository.md`, `releases.md`, `dependencies.md`), together with the exceptions register, then every source file in scope.
- Each scores every criterion from 1 to 10 and lists findings with a severity and a `file:line` citation. A finding must be verified by reading the code, not inferred from a name. Reviewers say what they did not read.
- Afterwards the reviewer's findings are compared with the open findings below; anything still open that the reviewer missed is put to them before the score stands.

### What a severity means

| Severity | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| High | Can lose or duplicate a print, expose a credential or cross a security boundary; or the structure prevents a 2.0 requirement from being built or tested. |
| Medium | Wrong behavior in a case that can be reached but is uncommon; or a requirement of the standards that is not met. |
| Low | A local quality or consistency problem with no effect on behavior. |
| Structural | A measurement of size or shape recorded as evidence for a design score; closed when the structure changes. |

The reviews of individual changes use blocker, should-fix and nit. A should-fix carried into this file counts as Medium and a nit as Low.

### What a score means

A score follows from the findings against that criterion, so two reviewers with the same findings give the same score.

| Score | Findings against the criterion |
| --- | --- |
| 10 | None. |
| 9 | No High or Medium. Low findings only, few and not a pattern; every unmet requirement is covered by a recorded exception. |
| 7–8 | No High. One or two Medium (8), or several (7). |
| 5–6 | One High (6), or more than one, or Medium findings that form a pattern (5). |
| 3–4 | The criterion's main requirement is not met: for example, logic that cannot be tested without the interactive desktop. |
| 1–2 | Nothing in place. |
